The two 5.1kΩ pull-down resistors on CC1 and CC2 are mandatory — they tell the USB-C source this is a "Sink" device wanting 5V. Without them, many USB-C chargers will not supply power. Bulk decoupling: 10µF + 100nF ceramic on VBUS.

R_PROG sets charge current: I = 1200/R_PROG (mA). For 1A use 1.2kΩ. For 500mA use 2.4kΩ. The TEMP pin should be tied to VCC via 10kΩ if no NTC thermistor is fitted (disables thermal monitoring). CHRG and STDBY are open-drain outputs — pull high to VCC via 1kΩ LED + resistor combos.

Use a quality cell (Samsung 25R, LG HG2, Sony VTC5). At 500mA load the LDO needs ~750mA from the cell (accounting for its efficiency). This is well within any 18650's rating. Runtime at 500mA continuous would be roughly 3–5 hours depending on cell.

DW01A + FS8205A — Cell Protection

The DW01A monitors cell voltage and current. It drives the gate of two back-to-back N-channel MOSFETs (FS8205A) in the negative battery rail. One FET controls the charge path, the other the discharge path — preventing both overcharge and over-discharge independently. This is the standard protection circuit used in most commercial 18650 packs.

The AP2112K is a good choice for this design — 600mA headroom above your 500mA burst, low dropout means it works until the protection IC trips at 2.4V cell voltage (well above the 3.55V needed). Add 10µF on the output specifically to handle 500mA burst transitions without voltage dip. Enable pin can be tied to VIN for always-on.

Design Notes

⚡ Charging Topology

The TP4056 is a linear charger — it dissipates (Vin − Vbat) × Icharge as heat. At 1A charge current and early in the charge cycle (Vbat = 3.0V), that's 2W of heat in the IC. Ensure adequate copper pour under the exposed pad or reduce charge current to 500mA (R_PROG = 2.4kΩ) if thermals are tight.

🛡 Protection Topology

The DW01A + FS8205A sit in the negative battery rail. Both the charge and discharge paths run through the back-to-back FETs. This allows the IC to independently block charging (OVP), discharging (UVP), or both (OCP). The 10mΩ sense resistor causes only 5mV drop at 500mA — negligible.

🔋 LDO Dropout

The AP2112K needs Vin ≥ 3.55V to maintain 3.3V output at 600mA. The cell's protection cutoff is 2.4V — so the LDO will droop before the protection trips. Consider adding a low-battery indicator (comparator) at ~3.6V if reliable regulation to discharge is important.

🔌 USB-C CC Resistors

The 5.1kΩ pull-downs on CC1 and CC2 are not optional. USB-C PD sources use these to detect the load type. Without them, compliant chargers may refuse to supply current or limit to 100mA. Use 1% tolerance resistors placed close to the USB-C connector.

🌡 Thermal Management

Place the TP4056 away from the battery holder. Maximum ambient for safe Li-Ion charging is typically 45°C. If the board will operate in warm environments, add the optional NTC thermistor on the TEMP pin — the TP4056 will automatically throttle charge current when hot.

📐 PCB Layout Tips

Keep the charge current path (USB-C → TP4056 → Battery) with wide traces (≥1mm). The LDO output 500mA path also needs ≥0.5mm traces. Place decoupling caps within 1mm of their IC's power pins. Separate analog ground (LDO, protection) from the high-current charge ground if possible.
