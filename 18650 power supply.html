Your module contains the full charge and protection circuit. Always connect the LDO to OUT+/OUT−, not to B+/B−. The OUT terminals are on the load side of the protection FETs — connecting directly to B+/B− would bypass the overdischarge protection entirely. The module's on-board USB-C socket handles CC resistors and polyfusing internally; no additional input-side components are needed on your PCB.

The XC6220B331MR-G offers 700mA continuous output — 200mA headroom over the 500mA burst requirement. Its 200mV dropout means it stays in regulation down to ~3.50V on the OUT+ rail. The CE (enable) pin must be tied to VIN — do not leave it floating. Add 1µF on the input and 10µF on the output; the output cap is important for absorbing 500mA burst transients cleanly.

Design Notes

⚠ OUT+ not B+

Always connect your PCB to the module's OUT+/OUT− terminals, never directly to B+/B−. OUT+ is on the load side of the protection FETs. Connecting to B+/B− bypasses overdischarge protection — the cell could be drained below 2.4V and permanently damaged.

🔋 LDO Dropout

The XC6220B needs Vin ≥ 3.50V to maintain 3.3V at 700mA. The module's DW01A trips at 2.4V — so the LDO will droop well before the protection cuts in. Consider a low-battery LED (comparator at ~3.6V) if regulated output to deep discharge matters. The CE pin must be tied to VIN — do not leave it floating.

📐 PCB Connections

The module typically exposes four pads: IN+/IN− (USB-C power, usually not broken out), B+/B− (battery), and OUT+/OUT−. Your PCB needs only two wires or header pins to OUT+/OUT−. Use ≥0.5mm traces for the OUT+ rail; at 500mA burst the trace resistance matters.

🔌 Module Mounting

The module can be soldered directly to your PCB via its through-hole pads, or mounted off-board and connected via short wires. Keep OUT+ wiring short to minimise series resistance and inductance. If the module sits on your PCB, ensure the 18650 holder weight is mechanically supported — don't rely solely on the solder joints.

🌡 Thermal

The TP4056 on the module dissipates (5V − Vbat) × Icharge as heat — up to 2W at the start of a charge cycle. Ensure the module has airflow around it. If your enclosure is sealed and small, consider the module's charge current — many Amazon variants have a 1kΩ R_PROG for 1A; replacing it with 2kΩ halves the heat at the cost of longer charge time.

⚡ Output Capacitors

Place the XC6220B's 1µF input cap and 10µF output cap as close as possible to the IC — within 1mm if you can. The 10µF output cap is specifically important for your 500mA/15s bursts; it supplies initial charge while the LDO's control loop responds. A parallel 100nF handles high-frequency noise.
