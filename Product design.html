01

Module 01 · 2–3 weeks

Understanding Users & Problems

Before designing anything, learn to ask: who is this for, and what frustration are they currently living with?

What you'll learn

  • The difference between a user need and a product feature
  • How to observe and interview potential customers to surface real problems
  • Jobs-to-be-Done theory — people don't buy products, they 'hire' them for a job
  • How to map user journeys to find pain points worth solving

📚 Books

The Design of Everyday Things
Don Norman · 2013 revised edition

The classic foundation of human-centred design. Norman explains why some products feel intuitive and others cause frustration — using everyday objects as examples. Introduces affordances, signifiers, feedback loops, and mental models.

Why it matters for you: Reading this changes how you look at every object around you. You'll start seeing why certain 3D-printed items feel satisfying and others get put in a drawer.

When Coffee and Kale Compete
Alan Klement · 2018 (free online)

A practical guide to Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) theory from the ground up. Reframes product thinking around the 'job' a customer is trying to accomplish in their life, not the features of the thing they buy.

Why it matters for you: Instead of thinking "I'll print a phone stand," you think: "What job is someone hiring a phone stand to do?" Shifts guessing to purposeful design. Available free online.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶IDEO "Design Thinking" explainer series— IDEO invented modern design thinking; short, accessible videos on empathy-first design
  • ▶AJ&Smart "What is Design Thinking?" and Sprint methodology videos— clear, practical explanations of user research and problem framing
  • ▶Design Plus Morna Videos on designer mindset and principles— Canadian industrial designer focused on how to think like a designer

🛠 Practical Exercise

Pick one item you currently sell. Find 3 people who own or might want it. Ask them: "Tell me about the last time you needed something like this — what were you trying to do?" Don't show them your product. Just listen. Write down every frustration and goal they mention.

02

Module 02 · 2–3 weeks

Market Research & Opportunity Spotting

Learn to look at what's already selling, find the gaps, and validate demand before spending a minute in your slicer.

What you'll learn

  • How to read Etsy and Amazon reviews as a research tool (the "review mining" technique)
  • How to use search trends and keyword tools to gauge real demand
  • How to spot underserved niches by looking at complaints in existing products
  • The difference between a market gap and a market that simply doesn't exist

📚 Books

Obviously Awesome
April Dunford · 2019

A no-fluff guide to positioning — the art of making your product obviously the right choice for the right customer. Dunford explains how to find your competitive alternatives, identify what genuinely differentiates you, and match that to the customers who care most.

Why it matters for you: Helps you stop competing on price and start carving out a niche where you're the obvious answer. Directly applicable to how you describe and position products in your Etsy shop.

Competing Against Luck
Clayton Christensen et al. · 2016

Christensen expands Jobs-to-be-Done into a full business framework, with real company case studies showing how understanding the 'job' customers hire products for leads to innovation that sticks.

Why it matters for you: Ideal companion to Module 1. Helps you see how to systematically look for underserved jobs in your product category.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶Jungle Scout Etsy product research and niche-finding tutorials— practical techniques for reading market data on Etsy and Amazon
  • ▶Niche Pursuits Keyword and demand research tutorials— finding product niches with real, measurable demand
  • ▶The Futur Videos on finding your creative niche and positioning— design business channel; great on how to differentiate in a crowded market

🛠 Practical Exercise

Go to Etsy. Find the top 5 sellers in your product category. Read their 1-star and 2-star reviews carefully. Make a list of every complaint. Those complaints are unmet needs — and potential product ideas.

03

Module 03 · 2–3 weeks

Design Principles & Form

Learn the timeless principles behind why some products feel well-made and others feel cheap — and how to apply them to physical objects.

What you'll learn

  • Core visual principles: proportion, balance, hierarchy, contrast, and rhythm
  • Why simplicity increases perceived value
  • How material, texture, and finish affect how people feel about a product
  • The psychology of aesthetics — why certain forms feel satisfying

📚 Books

The Laws of Simplicity
John Maeda · 2006

A short, elegant book that distils ten laws for simplifying design across products, technology, and business. Maeda argues that great design is about strategic reduction, not addition.

Why it matters for you: Directly applicable to 3D printing. The tendency in CAD is to add — features, details, complexity. This book teaches you when and how to take away instead.

100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People
Susan Weinschenk · 2011

Grounded in cognitive and behavioural science, this book explains how people actually see, think, feel, and decide. Covers perception, attention, memory, motivation, and social behaviour — all through the lens of design.

Why it matters for you: Helps you understand why certain colours, shapes, and forms attract attention or signal quality. Immediately useful for both product design and product photography.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶The Design Sketchbook Industrial design principles and sketching fundamentals— teaches form language and how to think about 3D shapes
  • ▶John Mauriello Design Design principles, form language, and product analysis— industrial design professor; deep dives into why products look and feel the way they do
  • ▶Crash Course Design Visual design principles series— short, accessible introductions to proportion, colour, contrast, and composition

🛠 Practical Exercise

Pick a household object you admire. Write down 10 specific reasons why it feels well-made. Then pick something that feels cheap or frustrating. Write 10 reasons for that too. You're training your design eye — and this gets surprisingly powerful quickly.

04

Module 04 · 2 weeks

Ideation & Concept Development

Move from a vague idea to a concrete concept worth printing — using structured techniques to generate, filter, and develop ideas.

What you'll learn

  • How to use "How Might We" questions to reframe problems as opportunities
  • Brainstorming and SCAMPER techniques for physical products
  • How to evaluate ideas using desirability, feasibility, and viability
  • Creating rough concept sketches to compare ideas before you model anything

📚 Books

Creative Confidence
Tom & David Kelley · 2013

Written by the founders of IDEO, this book argues that creativity is a skill anyone can build — not a talent you either have or don't. Provides practical techniques for generating ideas and overcoming the fear of being wrong.

Why it matters for you: Gives you concrete tools to deliberately generate more and better ideas, and to feel confident experimenting — rather than waiting for inspiration.

Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products
Nir Eyal · 2014

Eyal introduces the Hook Model — a four-stage cycle (trigger, action, reward, investment) explaining why some products become indispensable while others are forgotten. Rich with examples from physical and digital products.

Why it matters for you: Helps you design products people keep using, come back to, and recommend. Useful for thinking about repeat purchases and product ecosystems.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶IDEO Ideation and brainstorming technique videos— the originators of design thinking show you structured ideation methods used by professionals
  • ▶AJ&Smart Design Sprint process videos — ideation phase— practical walkthroughs of how teams generate and filter concepts quickly
  • ▶Product Design Online Concept development in Fusion 360 for makers— bridges design thinking and CAD, relevant to 3D printing practitioners

🛠 Practical Exercise

Take one of the unmet needs you found in Module 2. Set a timer for 10 minutes. Generate 20 different product concepts that could solve it — including wild, impractical ones. Mark your top 3 and sketch each one roughly on paper before opening any CAD software.

05

Module 05 · 2–3 weeks

Rapid Prototyping & Iteration

You already have the best rapid prototyping tool available — your printer. This module teaches you how to structure your test cycles to learn faster and waste less.

What you'll learn

  • The Build-Measure-Learn loop from Lean Startup methodology
  • How to define what you're testing before you hit print
  • The difference between concept tests, usability tests, and market tests
  • How to get useful feedback from potential buyers without giving the product away

📚 Books

The Lean Startup
Eric Ries · 2011

The definitive guide to validated learning — testing your assumptions about what customers want before investing heavily in building it. Built around the Minimum Viable Product concept and the Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop.

Why it matters for you: The core principle maps perfectly to 3D printing: print the minimum version that tests your key assumption, get feedback, improve. Stops you building full production runs of things nobody wants.

Prototyping and Modelmaking for Product Design
Bjarki Hallgrimsson · 2012

A practical, visual guide to prototyping physical products — covering sketch models, appearance models, functional prototypes, and how to plan a prototyping strategy. Written specifically for industrial and product designers.

Why it matters for you: The only book on this list written specifically about physical prototyping. Highly practical for someone with 3D printing capability.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶Y Combinator Talks on MVP, user testing, and iteration by founders— hard-nosed startup thinking on how to test ideas before committing resources
  • ▶Product Design Online Iteration and refinement in Fusion 360— shows how to build iterative design thinking into your CAD workflow
  • ▶Evan & Katelyn Making-and-selling process vlogs— popular maker couple who openly share their product development and testing process

🛠 Practical Exercise

Before your next product print, write down: (1) What am I assuming people will like about this? (2) What's the fastest way to test that assumption? (3) What would tell me this design is failing? Then run the test with at least two real people before committing to production.

06

Module 06 · 2 weeks

Pricing, Value & Positioning

Understand the psychology behind perceived value — and learn to design and present products so that your price feels entirely reasonable.

What you'll learn

  • Why cost-plus pricing undervalues most handmade products
  • Value-based pricing — pricing to the customer's perceived benefit, not your material cost
  • How design quality, packaging, photography, and copy all signal value
  • How to position a product so the right buyer self-selects

📚 Books

Obviously Awesome (revisited)
April Dunford · 2019

Referenced in Module 2, but worth revisiting here for its insights on communicating value. Dunford's positioning framework directly informs how you write product descriptions, choose photos, and set prices in your shop.

Why it matters for you: Re-read with a focus on the chapters about value and competitive alternatives. You'll see your listings quite differently.

Building a StoryBrand
Donald Miller · 2017

Miller argues that customers don't buy the best product — they buy the product they can understand fastest. Provides a seven-part narrative framework for clarifying your message so buyers immediately grasp what you do and why it matters to them.

Why it matters for you: Immediately applicable to product titles, descriptions, and your shop's About page. Helps your listings communicate value in seconds rather than paragraphs.

🎬 Videos

  • ▶The Futur Pricing psychology and value-based pricing talks— excellent explainers on why undercharging is a design problem, not just a business one
  • ▶Etsy Seller Handbook Pricing strategies for handmade product sellers— platform-specific but grounded in real maker economics
  • ▶Alex Hormozi Value creation and offer framing content— blunt, practical content on how to frame and communicate what you sell

🛠 Practical Exercise

Take your best-selling product. Write a new description using the StoryBrand framework: (1) Who is the customer? (2) What problem do they have? (3) How does your product solve it? (4) What does their life look like after they buy it? Then A/B test the listing against your current one.

A Note on How to Use This

Don't wait until you've read everything before applying it. The best way to learn product design is to read a bit, make something, observe what happens, and come back to the reading with fresh questions.

You already have a significant advantage: you can print a prototype in hours. Most product designers would kill for that. Use the frameworks here to aim your printing at things more likely to resonate — and you'll find your hit rate improving noticeably.

Start with Module 1 and The Design of Everyday Things. Everything else flows from being able to see the world through a user's eyes.
